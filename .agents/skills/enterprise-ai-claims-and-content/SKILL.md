# Enterprise AI Claims and Content

Publish only what the new brand is authorized and able to substantiate.

## Verify before drafting

1. Classify the statement as product, customer, metric, security, privacy, compliance, certification, legal, competitive, or general editorial content.
2. Locate its approved claim record and authoritative evidence.
3. Verify the approved wording, owner, allowed surfaces, attribution, verification date, and expiry or revalidation requirement.
4. Confirm that the implementation does not broaden the meaning, audience, geography, time period, or guarantee.
5. Keep the claim identifier traceable from the content source without exposing confidential evidence publicly.

If no approved record exists, do not publish the claim. Mark draft copy as unverified or replace it with neutral, non-factual language and request approval.

## Protect trust

- Treat public TextCortex material as research context only. It is not transferable proof for the standalone brand without a new-brand approval record.
- Require explicit approval for customer names, logos, quotes, case-study facts, certifications, benchmarks, adoption figures, and savings claims.
- Preserve qualifiers, sample sizes, study dates, regions, and attribution required by the evidence.
- Do not imply a legal, security, privacy, or compliance guarantee.
- Apply the same policy to page copy, metadata, schema markup, image text, alt text, social cards, analytics labels, and generated content.
- Never place confidential source documents, customer information, personal data, or internal discussions in the public repository.

## Review before release

Check the rendered statement against the claim record, test its links and attribution, and confirm that expired or superseded evidence is not shipping. Use $enterprise-ai-release-audit for the final cross-site claims review.

Read [references/claims-registry-contract.md](references/claims-registry-contract.md) whenever creating, reviewing, or consuming a claim record.
